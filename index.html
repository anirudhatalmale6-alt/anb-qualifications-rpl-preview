Two qualifications

One gets you into the industry. The other moves you up in it.

CHC33021 — nationally recognised

Certificate III in Individual Support

To get into care work

The standard entry qualification for aged care, home care and disability support. This is the one most employers ask for before they will put you on.

Structure
15 units — 9 core and 6 electives
Work placement
120 hours. If you already work in care, your current work may count.
Specialisations
Ageing, Disability, or Ageing and Disability
Suits you if
You are new to care, or you have been doing the work for years without the paperwork to prove it.
CHC43015 — nationally recognised

Certificate IV in Ageing Support

To step up in aged care

The next level for people already working in aged care who want more responsibility — care planning, coordinating support, supervising and mentoring other workers.

Structure
18 units — 15 core and 3 electives
Work placement
120 hours. Your current aged care work may count.
Where it leads
Senior personal care worker, team leader, care supervisor and similar roles.
Suits you if
You are already working in aged care, ideally with a Certificate III behind you, and you want to move up.

Two ways to get there

Both qualifications can be done either way. Which one applies to you depends entirely on whether you are already working in the sector.

Recognition of Prior Learning

Already working in care
  • You are already doing the work — personal care, home care, aged care, disability support.
  • Instead of sitting the course, you prove what you already do: payslips, position description, a supervisor reference, work samples.
  • Triscott's assessor reviews your evidence and may have a short competency conversation with you.
  • Anything genuinely missing is filled with targeted gap training — just that part, not the whole qualification.
  • Usually the faster and cheaper route if your experience is current.

The full course

New to the industry
  • You complete all the units with Triscott — 15 for the Certificate III, 18 for the Certificate IV.
  • Includes the mandatory 120 hours of work placement in a real care setting.
  • Triscott arranges and supervises the placement.
  • You finish job-ready with the same nationally recognised qualification.
  • The right path if you have no paid experience in care yet.

Choose your specialisation

The Certificate III can be awarded with a specialisation shown on the certificate. It comes down to which electives you do. The Certificate IV is aged care specific, so it does not have specialisations.

Ageing

Residential aged care and home care for older people.

Disability

Supporting people living with disability, including NDIS settings.

Ageing and Disability

Both, which keeps the most doors open when you apply for work.

Common questions

Who actually gives me the certificate?

Triscott Educational Services Pty Ltd, RTO 6937. They do the assessing and issue the qualification. A&B First Aid Training refers and enrols you with them. We do not issue these qualifications — we only issue our own first aid and CPR certificates.

Is it the same certificate as going directly to Triscott?

Yes, identical. Same code, same RTO, same nationally recognised qualification. Going through us just means someone walks you through it instead of leaving you to work it out alone.

What does it cost?

You pay one fee to A&B First Aid Training, which covers your assessment with Triscott Educational Services and the support we give you in putting your evidence together. We confirm it in writing before you commit to anything.

I have been in aged care for years — do I really have to do the whole course?

Probably not. That is exactly what Recognition of Prior Learning is for. Do the pathway check above and we will tell you where you stand.

Should I do the Certificate III or the Certificate IV?

If you are not yet working in care, start with the Certificate III — it is the qualification employers ask for. If you are already working in aged care and want to move into care planning, coordination or supervising others, the Certificate IV is the step up. The check above will point you to the right one.

How long does RPL take?

It depends how quickly you gather your evidence. The assessment itself is not the slow part — collecting payslips, your position description and a supervisor reference usually is.
